# Chapter Five: Beneath the Streets

The darkness of the underground chamber seemed to breathe. Merry stood at the threshold, her fingers still gripping the black key, unable to move despite every instinct screaming at her to run. The pale eyes staring at her from the center of the darkness weren't cruel—they were ancient. Exhausted. Hungry.

"Don't move," Cassandra whispered behind her, so quietly that Merry could barely hear it. "Not yet."

The chamber walls were lined with symbols carved into stone so old it had begun to crumble. Merry's eyes adjusted slowly to the darkness, revealing more details with each second that passed. Shelves lined every wall, covered in objects she couldn't quite make out. Some glinted in what little light filtered through the doorway. Others seemed to absorb the light, creating darker pools of shadow.

The eyes moved. A shape shifted in the center of the chamber, and Merry gasped. It wasn't what she expected. The creature before her wasn't monstrous or grotesque. It was almost human—almost. Its skin was translucent, like paper held up to candlelight, and through it she could see the faint outline of veins pulsing with something that wasn't blood. The Eraser, she realized. This was the thing that had been hunting Bellweather for centuries.

"How long?" The voice came from everywhere and nowhere at once, echoing off the stone walls and seeming to originate from inside Merry's own chest. "How long has it been?"

Cassandra stepped forward, placing a hand on Merry's shoulder. "Fifty years, as always," she said, her voice steady despite the tremor Merry could feel in her grip. "The cycle continues."

"Fifty years of nothing," the Eraser continued, its voice like wind through a tomb. "Fifty years of darkness and hunger. And still, the key finds its way to someone new. Always someone new. Never the same hands twice."

Merry found her voice, though it came out smaller than she intended. "Why? Why do you come back?"

The creature moved closer, and Merry forced herself not to step backward. The floor beneath her feet felt unstable, as if the very foundation of Bellweather was shifting. "Because I was sealed here by those who feared what I know. Those who feared what I could teach them. They bound me beneath their streets and told their children that I was a monster. A shadow to fear."

"You destroyed memories," Cassandra said sharply. "You took entire families from people's minds. Entire lives erased."

"I showed them the truth," the Eraser replied. "That memory is a cage. That the past is a weight we choose to carry. I merely gave them freedom."

Merry's hand tightened around the key. She could feel its warmth now, spreading through her palm and up her arm like a living thing. "Iho gave this to me. Why?"

The Eraser's eyes fixed on her with renewed intensity. "Because the boy understands what many do not. That this cycle cannot continue. That one day, the key will not be found in time, and Bellweather will fall to darkness. Or that one day, someone will refuse to carry it."

The chamber walls seemed to pulse with the creature's words. Merry could see the symbols more clearly now, and she realized with a start that they told a story. Picture after picture carved into stone, showing generations of people holding the black key. Some stood defiant. Others knelt in surrender. A few appeared to be running.

"What does the key do?" Merry asked. "What does it really do?"

"It binds me," the Eraser said. "It tethers my hunger to a single person. The key's holder becomes my anchor in this world. As long as they live and hold it, my power is contained. But the moment they die or release it, I grow strong once more. The town has fifty years before another holder must be found."

Cassandra's grip on Merry's shoulder tightened. "That's why Iho gave it to you. Our family was supposed to find the next holder, but you found him instead. The key chooses."

"Or chooses for you," the Eraser said with something that might have been a laugh. "The boy understands the burden. He tried to spare you from it. He would have carried it himself, eventually, had you not stumbled into his path."

Merry looked down at the key in her hand. It felt heavier than before, as if all the weight of the responsibility it carried was suddenly pressing down upon her. Behind her, she could hear the normal sounds of Bellweather returning to the streets above. The market vendors calling their wares. Children laughing. The sound of the clocktower chiming the hour.

All of it oblivious to what lay beneath their feet.

"If I refuse the key," Merry said slowly, "what happens?"

The Eraser moved back into the center of the chamber, its translucent form becoming less distinct in the darkness. "Then you are free. And I am released. And everyone in Bellweather will experience the freedom of forgetting. Every painful memory, every loss, every sorrow—gone. They would live in perfect, eternal contentment."

"They would be empty," Cassandra whispered.

"Perhaps," the Eraser agreed. "But emptiness feels rather like peace."

Merry closed her eyes. She thought of Mr. Gable's note. His terror at seeing the shadow at the door. She thought of Iho, trying so desperately to pass on a burden that wasn't his to carry. She thought of Cassandra, and all the people she'd never met, living their lives unaware that each one of them was only alive because someone, somewhere, had chosen to hold the key.

She opened her eyes and looked at the Eraser.

"No," she said.
