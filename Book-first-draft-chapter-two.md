Chapter Two: Panic


...scream, but the sound died in her throat. The shadow on the floor didn’t belong to a person — there was no body blocking the morning sunlight at the door. 

It was just a shape, flat and ink-black, mimicry without substance. 

When the shadow raised its hand, the movement was silent, dead silent. The fly that had been buzzing a moment ago froze mid-air, suspended in a draft of sudden, biting cold. 
Merry stumbled backward, her heel catching on a stray stool. 

The wooden box of clockwork pieces slipped from her grasp, crashing loudly against the creaky floorboards.The shadow winced at the noise. It shuddered, its finger dropping from its lips, and began to stretch rapidly toward her ankles. 

"Do **not** let it see your shadow." 

Mr. Gable’s frantic warning flashed in her mind. Merry scrambled over the counter, wondering what _it_ meant, abandoning her dignity and the scattered brass gears. 

She dropped into the cramped space behind the cash register just as the shop’s main gas lamp flared violently and went out.

Darkness flooded the front room. In the dim light left by the street window, Merry pressed her back against the wooden cabinets. 

She held her breath, shoving her hands into her pockets to keep them from shaking.

Her fingers brushed against something freezing cold. The black key.

_The_ key. 

The metal seemed to thrum against her palm, radiating a chill that matched the sudden drop in the shop's temperature. Outside, the familiar, bustling sounds of Bellweather — the shouting merchants, the wheeling seagulls, the clatter of carts, the chit-chat of the residents — felt miles away. Inside, there was only the sound of Merry’s own racing heart. 

A soft _scrape_ echoed from the other side of the counter. It didn’t sound like footsteps. It sounded like paper sliding across a rough floor. 

The shadow was searching for her. If it rounded the counter, the ambient light from the front window would throw Merry's silhouette right into its path. She closed the curtains, although the light still shone through. 

Slowly, carefully, Merry pulled her hand out of her pocket, gripping the small black key. Along its stem, the carved letter S caught the faint glint of daylight. The key didn't just feel cold anymore; it felt heavy, pulling her hand downward toward the floorboards.

Directly beneath her, near the baseboard of the counter, was a small, brass-rimmed keyhole she had never noticed before. It was tucked into the dark corner where Mr. Gable kept his reserve ledgers.

The scraping grew louder. A dark, featureless silhouette began to curl over the top of the counter, blotting out the remaining light.

Merry didn't hesitate. She lunged forward, thrusting the black key into the hidden lock. It fit perfectly. With a heavy, metallic click, the floorboards beneath her shifted, and the back panel of the counter swung inward into total darkness.
