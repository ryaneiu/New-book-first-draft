# Chapter Four: Trapped?

Merry stuttered, "Why did you do that?". However, they didn't have time to continue the conversation, since a loud thud made them shudder. Merry looked at the floor, then saw her shadow pointing towards the corner.
Cassandra told her, or rather screamed, "*HIDE!!!* No questions asked". Merry did as she was told. The thump grew louder and louder. *Thump, thump, thud*.
"To the emergency exit!" Cassandra ordered.
Merry acquiesed, and went through the back door.
When they went out, it was *like* normal, but the shadows all froze in place.
Merry whispered under her breath, just soft enough to be out of earshot from others, but for her cousin to hear her,
"So if you have the key, your shadow gives you orders, but if you're exempt of it, your shadow freezes. Who is behind all of it?"

Cassandra replied, "The Eraser."

"Who's that?"

"An entity, in a faraway land. If disturbed, he gets control over the person's memories and shadows. The only known two protections are either the Shadow-key or defeat him. If you do the latter, the whole village of Bellweather goes back to normal, but if you have the former, only you get the protection."

Merry felt a shiver go down her spine.

"What was Iho's intention to give the parcel to someone else?"

"I have no clue."

"Well, we may or may never find out."

Merry continued, "How do you defeat him?"

Cassandra recited, "By day and night, our ancestors have tried to get rid of him, because he does a disturbance when he's hungry, which happens about once per fifty years."

That answer did not comfort Merry at all. If the Eraser was hungry only once every fifty years, then Bellweather had been living on borrowed time. Cassandra's voice lowered to a whisper, as if she were speaking to a grave rather than to her cousin. "The disturbance never begins with a scream. It begins with a memory. First a person forgets a name, then a face, then a whole morning. The shadows follow after that, and when they lose all shape, the Eraser slips in."

Merry's hand tightened around the black key in her pocket. It felt warmer now, as though it had been waiting for this moment. "Then Iho gave me this because he thought I was meant to stop it?" she asked.

Cassandra glanced around the alley, making sure no one was watching. "Maybe. Or maybe he was trying to keep it away from someone else. There are old stories about a person who keeps the key too long and begins to hear orders from their own shadow. That's what happens when the town starts to remember the wrong things."

"The wrong things?"

"Things no one should ever know. The foundations under Bellweather were built to contain something older than the clocktower itself. Our ancestors sealed what they could and hid the rest. The key is only a piece. If he wakes fully, he won't just erase memories. He will erase the town's sense of itself."

The bells above the market square gave a single chime, though the sky had not yet turned evening. Merry looked up at the dark little chapel roof and felt the world tilt beneath her. For a second, the shadows on the stone walls seemed to breathe.

Cassandra grabbed her sleeve and pulled her toward the narrow stairwell behind the bakery. "Come on. If the Eraser is hungry, he will be looking for the same thing our grandparents hid: the old clockwork under Bellweather. There are chambers beneath the streets where the town's original mechanisms still run. If he reaches them, the whole village will lose its balance."

Merry followed, though every step made her heart beat harder. Below the cobbled streets, the air smelled of old metal, dust, and rain. They passed doorways hidden in brickwork and shelves lined with tools no one in the town had used in generations. Each wall bore strange carvings: tiny clocks, crescent moons, and the same looping S that was stamped into the key.

"This was all built to keep him out," Cassandra whispered.

"And now someone opened it?"

"Or someone is trying to wake him."

At the bottom of the stair, they reached a rusted iron door with a keyhole shaped like a half-moon. The black key trembled in Merry's hand and slid into the lock without effort. There was no sound at first, only a pressure in the air, like the entire room had been holding its breath.

Then the door creaked open.

Inside, the darkness was not empty. It pulsed with moving shapes, as if the shadows themselves had taken on a life of their own. And in the center of the chamber, a pair of pale, unblinking eyes watched them from the dark.

Merry froze, and for the first time since this nightmare began, she truly understood that the town was not merely strange. It was waiting.

And beneath Bellweather, something old and hungry had begun to stir.
